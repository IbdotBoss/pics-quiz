## ICO: Overview of the GDPR (ICO)

Document version dated 07 July 2016, version 1.0.25. Written when GDPR was due in force 25 May 2018 and before the Brexit outcome was settled.

### Introduction and scope (ICO p3-4)
- Compared against the UK Data Protection Act 1998 (DPA) (ICO p3). Referendum on EU membership: 23 June 2016 (ICO p3). GDPR expected in force in UK 25 May 2018 (ICO p3).
- New features named: breach notification and data portability (ICO p3).
- GDPR applies to controllers and processors (ICO p3).
  - Controller says how and why personal data is processed; processor acts on the controller's behalf (ICO p3)
  - Processors have specific legal obligations, e.g. maintain records of personal data and processing activities; significantly more legal liability for a breach; new under GDPR (ICO p3)
  - Controllers are not relieved of obligations when a processor is involved; must ensure contracts with processors comply (ICO p3)
- Applies to organisations operating within the EU and organisations outside the EU that offer goods or services to EU citizens (ICO p3)
- Does not apply to: processing covered by the Law Enforcement Directive; processing for national security purposes; processing by individuals purely for personal/household activities (ICO p3-4)
- Personal data: definition more detailed; online identifier, e.g. an IP address, can be personal data (ICO p4)
- Applies to automated personal data and manual filing systems where personal data are accessible according to specific criteria (ICO p4)
- Pseudonymised (e.g. key-coded) data can fall within scope depending on how difficult it is to attribute the pseudonym to an individual (ICO p4)
- Sensitive personal data = "special categories of personal data" (Article 9); include genetic and biometric data where processed to uniquely identify an individual (ICO p4)
- Criminal convictions and offences data are not special categories but similar extra safeguards apply (Article 10) (ICO p4)

### Principles (ICO p5)
- New accountability requirement is the most significant addition; no principles on individuals' rights or overseas transfers (those are Chapter III and Chapter V) (ICO p5)
- Article 5: personal data shall be:
  - (a) processed lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner in relation to individuals
  - (b) collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes (archiving in the public interest, scientific or historical research, statistical purposes not incompatible)
  - (c) adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes
  - (d) accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; inaccurate data erased or rectified without delay
  - (e) kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary
  - (f) processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security, including protection against unauthorised or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage, using appropriate technical or organisational measures
- Article 5(2): "the controller shall be responsible for, and be able to demonstrate, compliance with the principles."

### Lawful processing (ICO p7-8)
- Need a legal basis before processing; document it; basis affects individuals' rights (e.g. consent gives stronger rights such as deletion) (ICO p7)
- Member states may add specific provisions for Articles 6(1)(c) and (e); relevant to public authorities and highly regulated sectors (ICO p7)
- Lawfulness of processing conditions (ICO p7):
  - 6(1)(a) Consent of the data subject
  - 6(1)(b) necessary for performance of a contract with the data subject or to take steps to enter into a contract
  - 6(1)(c) necessary for compliance with a legal obligation
  - 6(1)(d) necessary to protect the vital interests of a data subject or another person
  - 6(1)(e) necessary for performance of a task carried out in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority vested in the controller
  - 6(1)(f) necessary for the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or a third party, except where overridden by the interests, rights or freedoms of the data subject. Not available to processing by public authorities in the performance of their tasks.
- Special category conditions, Article 9(2) (ICO p8): (a) explicit consent; (b) employment, social security or social protection law, or collective agreement; (c) vital interests where data subject physically or legally incapable of consent; (d) not-for-profit body with political, philosophical, religious or trade union aim (members/former members only, no disclosure to third party without consent); (e) manifestly made public by the data subject; (f) establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims or courts acting in judicial capacity; (g) substantial public interest on basis of Union or Member State law; (h) preventative or occupational medicine, assessing working capacity, medical diagnosis, health or social care; (i) public interest in public health; (j) archiving in public interest, scientific and historical research, statistical purposes (Article 89(1))

### Consent (ICO p8-9)
- Both `consent' and `explicit consent' must be freely given, specific, informed and an unambiguous indication of wishes (ICO p8)
- Requires clear affirmative action. Silence, pre-ticked boxes or inactivity do not constitute consent (ICO p8)
- Must be verifiable: keep a record of how and when consent was given (ICO p9)
- Right to withdraw consent at any time (ICO p9)
- Existing consent under DPA or EC Data Protection Directive (95/46/EC) need not be refreshed if it meets GDPR standard (Recital 171) (ICO p9)
- If the standard cannot be met: find an alternative legal basis or cease / not start the processing (ICO p9)

### Children (ICO p9)
- Privacy notice for services offered directly to a child must be clear, plain, understandable by a child
- Information society service (online services targeted at children) needs parent or guardian consent
- Parental/guardian consent required for children aged 16 and under; member states may lower the age in law, not below 13
- Information society services = most internet services provided at the user's request and for remuneration
- Protection particularly significant for marketing and creating online profiles
- Parental consent not required for preventative or counselling services offered directly to a child

### Individuals' rights (ICO p11)
The GDPR provides these rights: 1. right to be informed; 2. right of access; 3. right to rectification; 4. right to erasure; 5. right to restrict processing; 6. right to data portability; 7. right to object; 8. rights in relation to automated decision making and profiling. (Eight rights.)

### Right of access (ICO p12-13)
- Entitled to: confirmation that data is being processed; access to personal data; other supplementary information (largely the privacy notice information, Article 15)
- Purpose: so individuals are aware of and can verify the lawfulness of processing (Recital 63)
- Copy free of charge; the 10 pound subject access fee under DPA removed (shown as a garbled character in the extract). Reasonable fee allowed when request manifestly unfounded or excessive (particularly repetitive), or for further copies; fee based on administrative cost
- Time: without delay and at the latest within one month of receipt; extend by a further two months if complex or numerous; inform the individual within one month and explain why
- Manifestly unfounded or excessive: charge a reasonable fee or refuse to respond; if refusing, explain why, inform of right to complain to the supervisory authority and to a judicial remedy, without undue delay and at the latest within one month
- Verify identity using "reasonable means"; electronic request gets commonly used electronic format
- Best practice: remote access to a secure self-service system (Recital 63)
- Large amounts of data: may ask individual to specify the information; no exemption for large amounts

### Right to be informed (ICO p14-15)
- Obligation to provide `fair processing information', typically a privacy notice; transparency
- Information must be concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible; written in clear and plain language, particularly if addressed to a child; free of charge
- Information to supply: identity and contact details of controller (and representative) and the data protection officer; purpose and legal basis; legitimate interests; categories of personal data; recipients; transfers to third country and safeguards; retention period; existence of each data subject right; right to withdraw consent; right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority; source of the data and whether publicly accessible; whether provision is statutory or contractual and consequences of failing to provide; existence of automated decision making including profiling
- When: data obtained directly: at the time the data are obtained. Not obtained directly: within a reasonable period, within one month; if used to communicate with the individual, at the latest at first communication; if disclosure to another recipient envisaged, at the latest before the data are disclosed

### Right to rectification (ICO p17)
- Entitled to rectification if data inaccurate or incomplete
- Inform third parties it was disclosed to, where possible; tell individuals about those third parties where appropriate
- Respond within one month; extend by two months if complex; if not acting, explain why and inform of right to complain to supervisory authority and judicial remedy

### Right to erasure (ICO p18-19)
- Also known as "the right to be forgotten"; not absolute
- Applies where: no longer necessary for original purpose; individual withdraws consent; individual objects and no overriding legitimate interest; unlawfully processed; must be erased to comply with legal obligation; processed in relation to offer of information society services to a child
- DPA limited erasure to processing causing unwarranted and substantial damage or distress; GDPR has no such threshold
- Can refuse where processed: to exercise right of freedom of expression and information; to comply with legal obligation for public interest task or official authority; for public health purposes in the public interest; archiving in public interest, scientific research, historical research or statistical purposes; exercise or defence of legal claims
- Children: special attention where child gave consent and later requests erasure (regardless of age at time of request), esp. social networks and forums (Recital 65)
- Must inform third parties of erasure unless impossible or disproportionate effort; online organisations that made data public must tell other processors to erase links, copies or replication
- Example: search engine delists results; media publisher need not erase article if freedom of expression exemption applies

### Right to restrict processing (ICO p20)
- Similar to DPA right to `block' or suppress; may store data but not further process; retain just enough to ensure restriction respected
- Required when: individual contests accuracy (restrict until verified); individual objected and you are considering whether legitimate grounds override; processing unlawful and individual opposes erasure and requests restriction; you no longer need the data but individual needs it for a legal claim
- Inform third parties unless impossible or disproportionate; inform individuals when lifting a restriction

### Right to data portability (ICO p21-22)
- Obtain and reuse personal data for own purposes across different services; move, copy or transfer easily from one IT environment to another
- Example: midata (UK banking), personal current account customers access transactional data, upload to price comparison website
- Applies only: to personal data an individual has provided to a controller; where processing is based on consent or for performance of a contract; when processing is by automated means
- Provide in structured, commonly used and machine readable form; open formats include CSV files; free of charge
- May need to transmit directly to another organisation if technically feasible; not required to adopt compatible systems
- Consider other individuals' rights if data concerns more than one individual
- Respond without undue delay and within one month; extend by two months if complex or numerous; inform within one month
- Article 29 Working Party to publish guidance

### Right to object (ICO p23-24)
- Right to object to: processing based on legitimate interests or task in public interest/official authority (including profiling); direct marketing (including profiling); processing for scientific/historical research and statistics
- Legitimate interests / public task: objection must be on "grounds relating to his or her particular situation"; stop unless compelling legitimate grounds that override, or for establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims
- Inform of right to object "at the point of first communication" and in privacy notice; "explicitly brought to the attention of the data subject and shall be presented clearly and separately from any other information"
- Direct marketing: stop as soon as you receive an objection; no exemptions or grounds to refuse; deal with at any time and free of charge
- Research: need "grounds relating to his or her particular situation"; if processing necessary for public interest task, no need to comply
- Online processing: must offer a way to object online

### Automated decision making and profiling (ICO p25-26)
- Right not to be subject to a decision when it is based on automated processing and produces a legal effect or similarly significant effect
- Must ensure individuals can: obtain human intervention; express their point of view; obtain an explanation of the decision and challenge it
- Does not apply if decision: necessary for entering into or performance of a contract; authorised by law (e.g. fraud or tax evasion prevention); based on explicit consent
- Profiling: any form of automated processing intended to evaluate certain personal aspects of an individual, in particular to analyse or predict performance at work, economic situation, health, personal preferences, reliability, behaviour, location or movements
- Safeguards: fair and transparent (meaningful information about logic, significance, consequences); appropriate mathematical or statistical procedures; technical and organisational measures to correct inaccuracies and minimise errors; secure data proportionate to risk and prevent discriminatory effects
- Automated decisions under Article 9(2) must not concern a child, or be based on special categories unless explicit consent or substantial public interest on basis of EU/Member State law

### Accountability and governance (ICO p27-33)
- Accountability principle in Article 5(2): demonstrate compliance; this is the controller's responsibility
- Privacy impact assessments and privacy by design now legally required in certain circumstances
- Demonstrate compliance: appropriate technical and organisational measures (staff training, internal audits, review of HR policies); maintain documentation; appoint DPO where appropriate; data protection by design and by default; DPIAs where appropriate; can also adhere to approved codes of conduct / certification
- By design and default measures: data minimisation; pseudonymisation; transparency; allowing individuals to monitor processing; creating and improving security features on an ongoing basis
- Records of processing: more than 250 employees must keep internal records; fewer than 250 employees only records for higher risk processing (risk to rights and freedoms; special categories or criminal convictions and offences) (ICO p28)
- Record: name and details of organisation (and other controllers, representative, DPO); purposes; categories of individuals and personal data; categories of recipients; transfers to third countries and safeguards; retention schedules; technical and organisational security measures. Similar to "registrable particulars" under the DPA. May have to be made available to the supervisory authority.
- DPIA = data protection impact assessment, also known as privacy impact assessment (PIA); not a legal requirement under the DPA (ICO p29)
- Must carry out DPIA when using new technologies and processing likely to result in high risk to rights and freedoms. High risk includes: systematic and extensive processing incl. profiling and decisions with legal or similarly significant effects; large scale processing of special categories or criminal convictions data; large scale, systematic monitoring of public areas (CCTV) (ICO p29)
- DPIA contents: description of operations and purposes (incl. legitimate interests); assessment of necessity and proportionality; assessment of risks to individuals; measures to address risk including security and to demonstrate compliance; can address more than one project (ICO p30)
- Must appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) if you: are a public authority (except courts in judicial capacity); carry out large scale systematic monitoring of individuals (e.g. online behaviour tracking); carry out large scale processing of special categories or criminal convictions and offences data (ICO p30)
- One DPO may act for a group of companies or public authorities; any organisation can appoint a DPO
- DPO minimum tasks (Article 39): inform and advise on obligations; monitor compliance (incl. managing internal activities, advising on DPIAs, training staff, internal audits); be the first point of contact for supervisory authorities and individuals
- Employer duties: DPO reports to highest management level (board level); operates independently, not dismissed or penalised for performing task; adequate resources (ICO p31)
- DPO role can be given to an existing employee if no conflict of interests; can be contracted out externally; GDPR does not specify credentials but requires professional experience and knowledge of data protection law
- Codes of conduct and certification (ICO p31-33): not obligatory; benefits: improve transparency and accountability, mitigation against enforcement action, improve standards. Codes drawn up by trade associations or representative bodies; must be approved by the supervisory authority and, where cross-border, the European Data Protection Board (EDPB). Code signatories subject to mandatory monitoring by a body accredited by the supervisory authority; infringement may lead to suspension or exclusion and fine up to 10 million Euros or 2 per cent of global turnover
- Certification: issued by supervisory authorities or accredited certification bodies; valid for a maximum of three years; can be withdrawn; does not reduce data protection responsibilities; failure risks fine up to 10 million Euros or 2 per cent of global turnover; encouraged by member states, supervisory authorities, the EDPB or the Commission

### Breach notification (ICO p35-36)
- Duty on all organisations to report certain breaches to the relevant supervisory authority, and in some cases to affected individuals
- Personal data breach: a breach of security leading to the destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data; more than just losing personal data. Example: hospital patient health record inappropriately accessed
- Notify supervisory authority only where breach likely to result in a risk to rights and freedoms (e.g. discrimination, damage to reputation, financial loss, loss of confidentiality, significant economic or social disadvantage). Example: loss of customer details leaving people open to identity theft = notify; staff telephone list = normally not
- Notify individuals directly where breach likely to result in a high risk; threshold higher than for the supervisory authority
- Notification contents: nature of breach (categories and approximate number of individuals and records); name and contact details of DPO or other contact; likely consequences; measures taken or proposed
- Report within 72 hours of becoming aware; information can be provided in phases; public notification without undue delay if sufficiently serious
- Failing to notify: fine up to 10 million Euros or 2 per cent of global turnover

### Transfer of data (ICO p37-39)
- Restrictions on transfers outside the EU to third countries or international organisations; conditions in Chapter V
- Commission decision (Article 45): transfer where the Commission has decided the third country, territory, sector or international organisation ensures an adequate level of protection
- Appropriate safeguards (Article 46): legally binding agreement between public authorities; binding corporate rules; standard data protection clauses adopted by the Commission; standard clauses adopted by a supervisory authority and approved by the Commission; approved code of conduct; approved certification mechanism; contractual clauses authorised by the supervisory authority; provisions in administrative arrangements between public authorities
- Derogations (Article 49): informed consent; performance of contract with individual or pre-contractual steps; contract in the interests of the individual between controller and another person; important reasons of public interest; legal claims; vital interests where data subject incapable of consent; made from a public register. First three are not available to public authorities in exercise of public powers
- One-off transfers allowed only if: not by a public authority exercising public powers; not repetitive; limited number of individuals; necessary for compelling legitimate interests of the organisation (not overridden); suitable safeguards. Must inform the supervisory authority and give additional information to individuals

### National derogations (ICO p40)
- Article 23: Member States may restrict transparency obligations and individual rights to safeguard: national security; defence; public security; prevention, investigation, detection or prosecution of criminal offences; other important public interests (economic or financial, budgetary, taxation, public health and security); protection of judicial independence and proceedings; breaches of ethics in regulated professions; monitoring, inspection or regulatory functions; protection of the individual or rights and freedoms of others; enforcement of civil law matters
- Chapter IX: freedom of expression and freedom of information; public access to official documents; national identification numbers; employee data; archiving, scientific or historical research and statistical purposes; secrecy obligations; churches and religious associations
