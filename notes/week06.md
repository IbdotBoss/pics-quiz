## Learning objectives (L6 s2)
- define what ethics are; define what ethics are not; explain how ethics apply to professionalism.

## What are ethics? (L6 s3)
- Ethos [Greek] meaning custom or character (noun).
- sociology: underlying sentiment informing beliefs, customs, practices.
- philosophy: science of the character of a group or individual.
- science: the moral element determining a character's action rather than his/her thought or emotion.
- Source: dictionary.com

## What are ethics not? (L6 s4)
- Ethics are: not necessarily the law; not the same for everyone.

## Where do ethics come from? (L6 s5)
- Hippocrates may have been the first proponent of ethical behavior.
- Sources: Supernaturalism, Intuitionism, Consequentialism, Exemplars, Ideologies.

## A little white lie (L6 s6)
- Should I be able to lie on my computer experience / my qualifications / my degree classification to get a job?
- Kant's axe; Trolley problem (BBC programmes).

## Before you make an (ethical) decision (L6 s8) - in order
1. Clearly identify all the facts
2. Define the areas of potential conflict
3. Identify the stakeholders
4. List all options
5. List the consequences of the actions
6. Now make the decision

## Basic concepts in ethical decisions (L6 s9)
- Responsibility; Accountability; Liability; Due Process; Legal Principle.
- Utilitarianism Principle: take the decision for the greatest good of everyone.
- Risk Aversion Principle: take the decision that causes least harm.
- Rule of Universality: if the action is not right for everyone, then it is not right for anyone (Kant, 1724-1804).
- Rule of Change: if an action cannot be taken repeatedly, then it is not right to take it at all (Descartes, 1596-1650).

## Ethical pressures (L6 s10)
- Potential areas for conflict: self, manager, client, organization, family, peer group.

## "Murder is bad" - ethical positions (L6 s11) (Source: BBC 2014)
- moral realism: "it is wrong to murder"
- Subjectivism: "I disapprove of murder"
- Emotivism: "down with murder"

## What are morals? (L6 s12)
- Morals are firmly set with the principles of right and wrong actions (good/bad, ying/yang).
- A person's morals can be summed up as "the collection of the environmental factors they are exposed to and what they have taken away from them."

## Prisoner theory / Prisoner's Dilemma (L6 s13-19)
- Two members of a Cyber gang are arrested and detained; solitary confinement, no means of communicating. (s14)
- Prosecutors lack sufficient evidence to convict on the principal charge of hacking; enough to convict both on a lesser charge. Each is offered a bargain: betray the other (testify the other committed the crime) or cooperate (remain silent). (s14)
- A and B each betray the other: each serves two years. (s15)
- A betrays B but B remains silent: A set free, B serves three years (and vice versa). (s15)
- Both remain silent: both only serve one year (on the lesser charge). (s15)
- Implied: no opportunity to reward or punish partner other than the sentences; decision will not affect future reputation. (s15)
- Game: you are detainee #A, lecturer is #B; 10 minutes to give an answer; write down the reason. (s13)
- Prisoner theory shows that two completely rational people might not cooperate, even when it is in their best interests to do so (Kuhn, 2014). (s17)
- Purely rational people (looking out for #1) will betray the other, meaning the only possible outcome is betrayal. (s19)
- Overall you could both get a lower sentence and both save face if you both stay silent (Holodny, 2016). (s19)
- Ethics/Morals/Law: given your answer, what did you stick to and which did you break? (s20)

## Ethical consultants in IT (L6 s21)
- Takes assignments only in those fields where they can confidently predict that they can help their client;
- Is professionally qualified in these areas and keeps up their CPD;
- Acts as a guide or mentor to junior or less experienced consultants;
- Has professional indemnity and public liability insurance as necessary;
- Subscribes to a professional body which has a code of professional conduct;
- Supports their professional body with their time or 'pro bono' consulting as appropriate.

## Ethical issues in IT (L6 s22)
- Intellectual Property Rights: copyright, ownership, how to reward the authors; software theft, illicit copying, spurious backups.
- Social impacts: Quality (how good is good enough?); Employment (unemployment, under employment, deskilling); Digital divide (the haves and have-nots).
- Privacy: Big Brother (data mining, web surveillance, facial recognition, customer profiling); Your boss is watching (monitoring access to data on networks).
- Artificial Intelligence (www.turing.ac.uk).

## Ethical issues in business (L6 s23) (Source: Josephson, 2015)
- Personal values: integrity, honesty, quality, loyalty; professionalism, responsibility; mutual respect, fairness and meritocracy; leadership, responsibility; client focus and teamwork.
- Business ethics involves adhering to legal, regulatory, professional and company standards, keeping promises, commitments and abiding by general principles like fairness, truth, honesty and respect.

## Ethical issues in research (L6 s24)
- For the "greater good"; safe and controlled.
- When participants are involved:
  - consent - informing participants
  - deception - withholding information
  - withdrawal - at any time without penalty
  - confidentiality - in accordance with DPA/GDPR
  - protection - at all times

## Summary (L6 s25)
- Ethics and morals are at times used interchangeably, but have two very different meanings.
- "If you get arrested, you now know who to trust (and who to not!)"
