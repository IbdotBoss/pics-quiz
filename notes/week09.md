# Week 9 - Lecture 9: Professionalism in IT (MOD002647)

## Professions (L9 s3) (Bott 2014; PIIIT 2014, chp 2)
- Bott (2014) describes professions as including doctors, lawyers, accountants, architects, IT professionals.
- Things in common:
  - substantial education and training in order to practise their profession;
  - the profession is organised into one or more professional bodies;
  - members of the profession are expected to conduct their activities in accordance with codes of conduct laid out by the professional bodies;
  - the professional bodies themselves decide the nature of training and control entry to the profession.

## Skilled trades or professionals? (L9 s4-7)
- Electricians vs Electrical engineers; Plumbers vs Mechanical engineers; Builders vs Civil engineers.
- Computer programmer vs Software Engineer; Cisco hardware installer vs Cisco Certified Engineer.

## What makes a job a profession? (L9 s8)
- skills are "important" to society
- skills are in short supply, or difficult to obtain
- job related certifications or examinations
- self regulation
- agreed body of shared knowledge
- membership of an overseeing body to be able to practice
- Professionalism is maintaining an ethical approach to one's job within the structure of an association (Adams & McCrindle, 2008).

## Results of having a profession (L9 s9)
- safer supply of service to the public?; give improved confidence to the public?; can charge more?; title (e.g. 'Dr' or letters after name).
- But is there a duty to: operate 'in public good'? give services in 'emergency'?

## Does it matter if we are IT professionals? (L9 s10-11)
- When we give advice; when we are designing safety critical systems.
- "The public are entitled to expect that the same professional disciplines apply in IT as in other professions such as medicine and law." (Roger Johnson, BCS President)

## If professionals break the rules (L9 s12)
- Should professionals have a legal duty to act with reasonable care?
- How to protect yourself as a professional: Professional indemnity insurance; CPD (Continuing Professional Development).

## UK professional bodies for IT (L9 s13)
- BCS (British computer society): founded 1957; approx. 100,000 members.
- IET (Institution of Engineering and Technology): founded in 2006; approx. 150,000 members.

## Other professional bodies for IT (L9 s15)
- IEEE (Institute for Electrical and Electronic Engineers): founded in 1946; over 100,000 members.
- ACM (Association of Computer Machinery): founded 1947; approx. 75,000 members.
- (L9 s24) OWASP: not-for-profit charity that promotes web security. CISCO: manufacturer and certification body. CREST: International not-for-profit accreditation and certification body.

## What defines a professional body? (L9 s16)
- professional examinations
- support for Continuing Professional Development through learning opportunities, and tools for recording and planning
- publish professional journals or magazines
- professional networks to meet and discuss their field of expertise
- issue a Code of Conduct, or ethics, to guide professional behaviour
- deal with complaints against professionals and implement disciplinary procedures
- advise governments and regulatory bodies
- be enabling fairer access to the professions
- provide careers support and opportunities for students, graduates and people already working

## BCS (The Chartered Institute for IT) Code of Conduct (L9 s17)
- sets the professional standards required as condition of membership
- applies to all members, irrespective of their membership grade
- governs the conduct of the individual
- Four sections: 1 Public Interest; 2 Professional Competence and Integrity; 3 Duty to Relevant Authority; 4 Duty to the Profession (Source: BCS, 2018).

### 1. Public Interest - You shall (L9 s18)
- have due regard for public health, privacy, security and wellbeing of others and the environment.
- have due regard for the legitimate rights of Third Parties.
- conduct your professional activities without discrimination on the grounds of sex, sexual orientation, marital status, nationality, colour, race, ethnic origin, religion, age or disability, or of any other condition or requirement.
- promote equal access to the benefits of IT and seek to promote the inclusion of all sectors in society wherever opportunities arise.

### 2. Professional Competence and Integrity - You shall (L9 s19)
- only undertake work that is within your professional competence.
- NOT claim any level of competence that you do not possess.
- develop your professional knowledge, skills and competence on a continuing basis, maintaining awareness of technological developments, procedures, and standards that are relevant to your field.
- ensure that you have the knowledge and understanding of Legislation and that you comply with such Legislation, in carrying out your professional responsibilities.
- respect and value alternative viewpoints and, seek, accept and offer honest criticisms of work.
- avoid injuring others, their property, reputation, or employment by false or malicious or negligent action or inaction.
- reject and will not make any offer of bribery or unethical inducement.

### 3. Duty to Relevant Authority - You shall (L9 s20)
- carry out your professional responsibilities with due care and diligence whilst exercising your professional judgement at all times.
- seek to avoid any situation that may give rise to a conflict of interest between you and your Authority.
- accept professional responsibility for your work.
- NOT disclose, or use for personal gain, confidential information except with the permission of your Authority, or as required by Legislation.
- NOT misrepresent or withhold information on the performance of products, systems or services (unless lawfully bound by a duty of confidentiality not to disclose such information), or take advantage of the lack of relevant knowledge or inexperience of others.

### 4. Duty to the Profession - You shall (L9 s21)
- accept your personal duty to uphold the reputation of the profession and not take any action which could bring the profession into disrepute.
- seek to improve professional standards through participation in their development, use and enforcement.
- uphold the reputation and good standing of BCS, the Chartered Institute for IT.
- act with integrity and respect in your professional relationships with all members of BCS and with members of other professions with whom you work in a professional capacity.

## ACM Code of Ethics (L9 s22-23) (Source: ACM, 2018)
### General ethical principles (s22)
- Contribute to society and to human well-being, acknowledging that all people are stakeholders in computing.
- Avoid harm.
- Be honest and trustworthy.
- Be fair and take action not to discriminate.
- Respect the work required to produce new ideas, inventions, creative works, and computing artefacts.
- Respect privacy.
- Honour confidentiality.
### Professional responsibilities (s23)
- Strive to achieve high quality in both the processes and products of professional work.
- Maintain high standards of professional competence, conduct, and ethical practice.
- Know and respect existing rules pertaining to professional work.
- Accept and provide appropriate professional review.
- Give comprehensive and thorough evaluations of computer systems and their impacts, including analysis of possible risks.
- Perform work only in areas of competence.
- Foster public awareness and understanding of computing, related technologies, and their consequences.
- Access computing and communication resources only when authorized or when compelled by the public good.
- Design and implement systems that are robustly and usably secure.
