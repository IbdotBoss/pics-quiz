### Data Protection overview (L2 s6)
- Data Protection Act 1984; updated in 1998, 2018: Data Protection Act 2018
- General Data Protection Regulation 2016
- They outline the areas in law which cover: the rights YOU have about YOUR data; the responsibilities others have to look after it carefully; 'privacy' you can expect around data storage

### Definitions (L2 s7-8)
- Data: raw facts gathered about someone or something (s7)
- Personal data: data about a living person who can be identified from the data; human beings (s7)
- Sensitive personal data: personal data related to racial or ethnical origins, religious believes, political opinions, physical/mental health, sexual preferences, criminal records (s7)
- Processing: anything you do with personal data: hold, store, gather, delete, modify... (s7)
- Data Subject: the individual who is the subject of personal data (s8)
- Data Controller: the person(s) (or organisation) who holds the data or controls the processing (s8)
- Data Processor: anyone in (an organisation) contracted to do some data processing under data controller's instructions (s8)

### DPA 1998 (L2 s9-10)
- Personal data held or processed in automated form AND some manual records (s9)
- Enforced by Information Commissioner (ICO) in UK (s9)
- 8 Data Protection Principles (s9)
- Data Controller obligated to (s10):
  - NOTIFY the Information Commissioner on: what TYPES of information held for what PURPOSES; obtained from WHO; disclosed to WHO
  - published on public Register
  - follow 8 Data Protection Principles
  - payment of annual fee

### Principles overview (L2 s11)
- DPA lays out 8 principles which apply to collecting and processing personal data
- Obligations on Data Controllers: Principles 1-5, 7, 8
- Rights of Data Subjects: Principle 6
- "compliance": just enough to comply with the law
- "best practice": good understanding within the law

### The eight principles
- Principle #1 (L2 s12): Personal data shall be processed fairly and lawfully
  - with consent
  - to perform a contract with individual
  - under a legal obligation
  - to protect the vital interests of the data subject
  - carry out public function under statutory powers
  - pursue legitimate Interests of data controller unless prejudicial interests of data subject
- Principle #1, sensitive data (L2 s13): needs extra care and higher security
  - with explicit consent
  - necessary under a legal obligation in the context of employment
  - to protect the vital interests of the data subject where consent cannot be given or is withheld
  - by certain not-for-profit bodies about their members (with appropriate safeguards)
  - where the information has been deliberately made public by data subject
  - in legal processes
  - to carry out certain public functions or under statutory powers
  - necessary for medical purposes, undertaken by a health professional
  - for racial or ethnic monitoring
- Principle #2 (L2 s14): Personal data shall be obtained for only for one or more specified and lawful purposes, and shall not be further processed in any manner incompatible with that purpose or those purposes
- Principle #3 (L2 s15): Personal data shall be adequate, relevant and not excessive in relation to the purposes for which they are processed
- Principle #4 (L2 s16): Personal data shall be accurate and where necessary kept up to date
- Principle #5 (L2 s17): Personal data ... shall not be kept any longer than is necessary for the purpose(s) it is processed
- Principle #6 (L2 s20) (Rights of a data subject): Personal data shall be processed in relation to the rights of data subjects under the Act. You have the RIGHT to:
  - access any data held on you (normally within 40 days, and fee up to £10)
  - know purpose(s) for which personal data held
  - have incorrect information corrected (or, if data controller won't correct, erased)
  - have any dispute over correctness recorded and visible with the record
  - an explanation behind the logic of automated decisions
  - request an assessment if DPA is being complied with
  - compensation for damage or damage and distress in (mis)use of personal data
  - prevent processing which causes substantial damage or substantial distress
  - not to receive direct marketing
- Principle #7 (Security) (L2 s18): Appropriate technical and operational measures shall be taken
  - against unauthorised or unlawful processing of personal data
  - against accidental loss, or destruction of, or damage to, personal data
- Principle #8 (Transfer Abroad) (L2 s19): Personal Data shall not be transferred to a country ... outside the EEA unless that country ... ensures an adequate level of protection for the rights and freedoms of data subjects in relation to the processing of personal data (putting on the internet = world-wide transfer)

### Exemptions (L2 s21)
- National Security
- Personal, Domestic and Recreational usage
- Prevention and detection of crime
- Journalistic, literature or artistic purposes
- Where another statute authorises disclosure

### Data Protection Act 2018 (L2 s22)
- URL: legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12 (ukpga_20180012)
- "This includes GDPR"
- GDPR: effective across all EU member states; provides legal obligations we must adhere to
- GDPR gives member states limited opportunities on how this applies to individual countries. The DPA 2018 takes care of this.
- Read the DPA2018 alongside GDPR to fully understand the legal obligations

### GDPR (L2 s23-24)
- General Data Protection Regulation; effective across all EU member states; supports the DPA2018 (s23)
- Main improvements: accountability principles: first, very clear that the data controller is responsible for compliance; second, the data controller must be able to demonstrate compliance (s23)
- Higher standard for consent: consent is now opt-in, and evidence must be kept (s23)
- Post-Brexit (s24): GDPR applies to all EU countries and citizens; we need to comply with GDPR; GDPR has extraterritorial effect, so non-EU countries are also affected, so any UK company doing business with the EU will still need to comply

## Reading: "User Data Privacy: Facebook, Cambridge Analytica, and Privacy Protection" (Isaak and Hanna, IEEE Computer, The Policy Corner, August 2018)

- Authors: Jim Isaak (IEEE Computer Society, past president) and Mina J. Hanna (Synopsys; chair IEEE-USA AI and AS Policy Committees); published by IEEE Computer Society, Computer, August 2018 (CA p56-59)
- Facebook gave unfettered and unauthorized access to PII (personally identifiable information) of more than 87 million Facebook users to Cambridge Analytica (CA p56)
- IoT devices: 500 million in 2003; 8 billion in 2017; expected 50 billion in 2020 (CA p57)
- Facebook being investigated by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) for violating a 2011 consent decree; the article says US privacy law is sorely deficient (CA p57)
- 2013: researchers at University of Cambridge's Psychometrics Centre analysed volunteers who took a personality test on Facebook to evaluate "OCEAN" profile (openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism), correlated with Facebook likes and shares; drew in 350,000 US participants (CA p57)
- OCEAN profile could be deduced reasonably accurately from these metrics without a formal psychographic instrument; no indication the research exposed participants or friends to privacy abuse; university reportedly refused to share data with what became Cambridge Analytica (CA p57)
- Second research project reportedly initiated by Global Science Research (GSR), in cooperation with Cambridge Analytica; personality quiz on Amazon's Mechanical Turk platform and Qualtrics (survey platform); quiz required users to grant GSR access to Facebook profile, which gave access to friends' data via the Facebook Open API until May 2015 (CA p57)
- Cambridge Analytica built "5,000+ data points on 230 million US adults"; ability to "micro-target" consumers or voters (CA p57)
- "Project Alamo": OCEAN analysis paired with targeted messages; used for the election campaign of President Trump (CA p57)
- CA CEO: identify those who might be enticed to vote for their client or discouraged to vote for opponent (CA p57)
- Not having a Facebook account gave no protection; websites with the Facebook logo link to Facebook, allowing tracking of non-members; web beacons tied to "cookies" (CA p58)
- Tipping the scale in a few states with as few as 100,000 voters using individualized messages is sufficient to impact election results (CA p58)
- Michal Kosinski (Stanford professor, part of 2013 Cambridge team) and colleagues: psychographic analysis significant impact with a sample base of 3.5 million users (CA p58)
- Core principles of privacy and data protection, based on IEEE-USA "Personal Privacy, Awareness and Control" position statement (CA p58-59), four headings:
  - Public transparency
  - Disclosure for users
  - Control (includes: "do not track" requests respected; users explicitly opt in to each data component retained; easily delete PII; disputes must not default to licenses and arbitration; consent for self must not extend to "friends" or "contacts"; minors protected by a legally mandated age of consent)
  - Notification (users directly and promptly informed of loss or misuse of private information; right to know source of violations and responsible parties; paid advertising and content must be clearly labelled as paid)
- CONSENT Act (S.2639): "Customer Online Notification for Stopping Edge-Provider Network Transgressions"; Senators Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) and Ed Markey (D-MA); requires FTC to establish privacy protections for customers of online edge providers; explicit opt-in consent before platforms use, share or sell PII; explicit notification any time data is gathered, shared or sold to a third party; new data breach reporting requirements; violations treated like unfair or deceptive acts under section 18(a)(1)(B) of the Federal Trade Commission Act, giving FTC jurisdiction (CA p59)
- Social Media Privacy Protection and Consumer Rights Act of 2018 (S.2728): Senator Amy Klobuchar (D-MN); similar constraints to CONSENT Act on disclosure and initial consent; adds restrictions on modifications to privacy terms, withdrawal of consent, and procedures on violation (notification, data erasure, ceasing to collect data) (CA p59)
- California: privacy bill to State Legislature; limits on selling data on users younger than 16 years of age; prevents businesses denying service to users who exercise their rights (CA p59)
- Debate started in April 2018 after the revelation; Mark Zuckerberg asked to testify before House Judiciary and Senate Judiciary and Commerce Committees (CA p59)
