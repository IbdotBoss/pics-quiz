### Climate change (L4 s5-s7)
- Since 1880, the average global temp has risen by 1.4 oC (L4 s6)
- International Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) predict (L4 s6):
  - 1.1 - 2.9 oC (lowest CO2 emission scenario)
  - 2.4 - 6.4 oC (highest CO2 emission scenario)
- Some possible climate effects (L4 s6):
  - rising sea levels => habitation / migration
  - increased power of storms => habitation / migration
  - changing rain distribution => food
- Slide 7 image source: Environment Agency, UK (L4 s7)

### Dual aspects for 'Green' computing (L4 s8-s9)
1) Computing can create efficiencies in the way we live (L4 s8): intelligent heating/lighting controls and sensors; management of Energy networks; reducing travel
2) Computing can be made more energy efficient (L4 s9): low power chips (RISC); solid state devices; low energy screens; better system power management; load balancing; lower power air conditioning

### How green is your cloud? (L4 s13-s14)
- Green cloud: resources are concentrated in more efficient data centres
- Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) = total facility power / IT equipment power
- Not so green cloud:
  - exponential growth far outstrips energy savings that can be obtained improving IT energy efficiency
  - large UPS systems take a lot of energy
  - not just about total energy, but what sort of energy is important: locating data centres near green power; selecting low carbon energy sources
  - approx 50% of costs are power related
  - SOURCE: Greenpeace, 2012
- Global Energy Use: source Enerdata, 2018 (image) (L4 s15)
- Data Centre Energy Use: source Greenpeace, 2012 (images) (L4 s17-s18)

### Statistics (L4 s16)
- Globally ICT uses more energy than everything in 1985
- ICT uses 10% of global power generation - 50% more than aviation (SOURCE: Digital Power Group, 2013)
- More internet traffic per hour than per year in 2000
- ICT will use 20% of global power by 2025 (SOURCE: The Guardian, 2017)
- IoT will produce 14% of global emissions by 2040 (SOURCE: The Guardian, 2017)

### Hidden consumption? (L4 s19)
- Average mobile phone customer uses 4.1 GB of data a month = 49.2 GBs per year
- Mobile Industry estimated 1GB requires 19 kW
- Annual Use = 934.8 kWh per year
- Connection costs about = 23.4 kWh per year
- Charging consumption = 3.5 kWh per year
- TOTAL = 961.7 kWh per year
- Sources: Statista, 2018; Walsh, 2013

### Precious metals (L4 s20)
- CRTs contain significant amount of lead
- LCD contains mercury
- Circuits - chromium, copper, lead
- Batteries - lead, nickel, cadmium, silver, lithium, etc.
- From 1 million recycled mobile phones we could recover: 35,274 pounds of copper; 772 pounds of silver; 75 pounds of gold; 33 pounds of palladium

### Green IT disposal (L4 s21)
- Licensed waste centre
- Duty of Care/Waste Transfer notices
- Certificate of Data Destruction
- Parts stripped for recycling: ferrous/non-ferrous materials; monitors (LCD, plasma, CRT); plastics (mice, keyboards, chassis)
- Donate - schools, charity, etc. (but not data storage devices)
- Data leakage (risk)

### Opportunities (L4 s22)
- better ways of doing things - redesign software, algorithms, hardware
- 'Green' collar jobs - green audits / monitoring / consultancy
- Special interest groups in IEEE, ACM, etc.
- Technology problems: replace old systems with more efficient ones; uses limited resources e.g. rare earth metals; batteries
- mobile device batteries double in efficiency over a 7 year cycle
- processing power doubles over a 2 year cycle
- Society is moving away from consumerism? (question mark on slide)

### Save energy (L4 s23)
- use device power management; sleep or off (still uses power); check power usage for your system; use laptops rather than desktops; re-new components rather than whole system; keep it cool - shade, ventilation; clean up unnecessary widgets and applications; switch off speakers and other peripherals when not in use; smart power strip, power monitor; minimise printing (2 sided)
